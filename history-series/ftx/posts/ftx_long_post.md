## Voiceover script
**hook:** In twenty twenty-two, a thirty year old was worth twenty-six billion dollars, on paper. He wore shorts and a T-shirt, played video games during investor meetings, and slept on a beanbag at the office. He shared the stage with former presidents and superstars. Then, in just one week, his empire collapsed, and about eight billion dollars of customer money was gone. So how did a guy in shorts make billions disappear? This is the story of FTX.

**sam:** Sam Bankman-Fried grew up in California. Both of his parents were law professors at Stanford. He was a math whiz who found school boring. He studied physics at MIT, then joined a Wall Street trading firm. He said he had one big goal: make as much money as possible, and then give it all away.

**alameda:** In twenty seventeen, he started his own crypto trading firm, called Alameda Research. He noticed that bitcoin cost more in Japan than in America. Buy cheap here, sell high there. He made millions. Then, in twenty nineteen, he launched FTX, an exchange where millions of people could buy and sell crypto. He moved the company to the Bahamas, and lived in a luxury penthouse with his closest colleagues.

**rise:** FTX grew incredibly fast. It paid one hundred thirty-five million dollars to put its name on the Miami Heat's arena. It ran a Super Bowl ad with Larry David. Tom Brady, Gisele, and Steph Curry all promoted it. Sam testified in front of Congress, and gave tens of millions of dollars to politicians. By early twenty twenty-two, investors valued FTX at thirty-two billion dollars. Magazines called him the next Warren Buffett. But behind the scenes, there was a secret.

**secret:** FTX and Alameda were supposed to be completely separate companies. But Alameda had a special privilege. It could borrow almost unlimited money from FTX, and nobody outside knew. And when crypto prices crashed in twenty twenty-two, Alameda was losing billions.

**collapse:** On November second, twenty twenty-two, a news site called CoinDesk published a leaked balance sheet from Alameda. Much of it was made of FTT, a token that FTX had created itself. People started asking: is the money really there? Four days later, Changpeng Zhao, the boss of the rival exchange Binance, announced he would sell all his FTT tokens. Panic. Customers rushed to pull out their money. About six billion dollars, in just seventy-two hours. Sam tweeted: FTX is fine. Assets are fine. He deleted it the next day.

**bankrupt:** FTX could not pay. It froze withdrawals. Binance offered to buy FTX, looked at the books, and walked away the next day. On November eleventh, FTX filed for bankruptcy, and Sam resigned. His fortune went from billions to almost nothing, in about a week.

**mess:** The man brought in to clean up the mess was John Ray, who had also cleaned up after Enron. Even he said he had never seen such a complete failure of corporate controls. Expenses were approved with emoji in group chats. There were no proper records of who owned what. So where did the money go? Prosecutors said customer money deposited at FTX had secretly been sent to Alameda, and used for risky trades, investments, political donations, and real estate in the Bahamas. About eight billion dollars of customer money was missing.

**trial:** In December twenty twenty-two, Sam was arrested in the Bahamas and flown to the United States. He was released on a two hundred fifty million dollar bond, and lived at his parents' house. But a judge sent him to jail before the trial, for trying to influence a witness. At the trial, his closest colleagues, including Alameda's boss Caroline Ellison, testified against him. Sam took the stand himself. Again and again, he said he could not remember.

**verdict:** In November twenty twenty-three, the jury found him guilty on all seven counts, after just a few hours. In March twenty twenty-four, he was sentenced to twenty-five years in prison. And the customers? Years later, most were told they would get back more than they had lost, in dollars. But crypto had soared since then. Many of them would have been far richer, if they had never trusted FTX.
